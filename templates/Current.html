DREAM FARM COMMONS

EXHIBITION BY JUDIT NAVRATIL
February 14 - 28

Curated by Selby Sohn + Your Mood Projects

14 February 5-8 pm
OPENING PERFORMANCE LECTURE AND DANCE WITH THE JUNCTION KEEP

28 February 4-6 pm
CLOSING CONVERSATION WITH VANESSA CHANG AND CASSIE THORNTON


Judit Navratil is presenting her PhD thesis in a solo show curated by Selby Sohn + Your Mood Projects at dream farm commons. The opening takes the form of Judit’s performance lecture, followed by a Valentine’s Day dance with The Junction Keep, a mobile public art experience that bridges safety, storytelling, and spirit across the Bay Area.

These past three years, Navratil has worked on her artistic research PhD, which she defended in a presentation in Vienna in December, successfully. Szívküldi Lakótelep: the urge for phygital, ludic communities traces her long-term, practice-based inquiry into digital belonging and topophilia. It engenders community-making across social VR through embodied, participatory forms. Judit’s PhD is framed by her experience as an immigrant in continuous transit and movement. Her Reflexive Document is structured as a series of Long Distance Somersaults moving through möbius loops, circling around questions of home, play and care across phygital space. The exhibition serves as the layered, spatial constellation. The base of this research unfolds as an embodied inventory that comes into full articulation through her performance lecture as a living, connective whole.

THE ANNEX

TRACES OF ELSEWHERE
March 7 - April 12

An exhibition by Maria Santos

7 March 6-9 pm
OPENING RECEPTION WITH LIVE SOUND BY AERIAL LOOP

12 April 3-5 pm
CLOSING WORKSHOP: MAPPING THE INVISIBLE


Maria Santos transforms the Annex into a meditation on displacement and return. Working across sculpture, textile, and video, Santos constructs fragile architectures from salvaged materials gathered during her travels through the Central Valley and along the US-Mexico border. Each object carries the residue of its origin — dust from agricultural fields, threads pulled from discarded work clothing, fragments of handwritten letters found in thrift stores.

The centerpiece of the exhibition is a suspended installation of woven copper wire and dried plant matter that maps the migratory routes of Santos's own family across three generations. Viewers are invited to walk beneath and around the piece, activating subtle sound elements triggered by proximity sensors embedded in the floor. The effect is both intimate and disorienting, as if stepping into someone else's memory.

A series of small video works projected onto irregular surfaces — a crumpled sheet of paper, a clay vessel, a folded shirt — extend the installation's concerns into the realm of moving image. Each video documents a simple, repeated gesture: hands kneading dough, feet crossing a threshold, fingers tracing the edge of a photograph. These loops resist narrative closure, instead offering glimpses of the rituals that sustain connection across distance.

Santos has exhibited internationally at venues including the Museo de Arte Contemporáneo de Oaxaca, the Berkeley Art Museum, and the Headlands Center for the Arts. This is her first solo exhibition at Dream Farm Commons.
